# Financial audit — Litt's Loans, 10 October 2026

**Scope:** every bank row in production (1,922 rows, 16 Jan 2025 → 9 Oct 2026, wallet 69811362),
the double-entry ledger, the loan and mortgage records, commissions, equity, and the figures the app
shows. **Read-only** — nothing was changed. Procedures re-runnable from `scripts/audit/`.

**Method** (ISA 500 assertions — existence, completeness, accuracy, cut-off, classification,
valuation): reconciliation to independent sources (the bank export header, the State's monthly tax
records, the tablet), recalculation of every instalment, levy, fee and kept amount from the
contract terms, reperformance of the app's own figures, journal-entry testing, and analytical
review. Differences that the data couldn't explain were traced to game behaviour.

## Opinion

**The books are accurate and complete.** Cash, receivables, tax and mortgages reconcile to the
dollar against independent evidence; every bank row is posted exactly once; the reports agree with
the ledger. Findings are about *classification and presentation* (which income line or which loan
something sits on) and about **game behaviour**, not missing or wrong money — except where noted.

## Findings

### F1 — Repeated legacy-mortgage banker fees (new game bug) · presentation · $132,000
In Jan–Feb 2026 the game charged the legacy-mortgage banker fee **again on each attempt**: Finley
Thornbury 10 × $8,000 in 31 minutes, Darcy Brightwater 6 × $8,000 in 17 minutes, Winter Calloway 2 × $10,000.
Staff refunded each extra charge within minutes (9 + 5 + 2 refunds, "Broker Fee Reimbursement",
"Broker Fee ISSUES ANGRY"). Net effect $0, but fee income (4200) and Refunds (5600) are **both
inflated by $132,000**. *Recommend:* book fee refunds against the fee income (a contra line) so
neither is overstated.

### F2 — Legacy-mortgage money filed as loan money · classification · ~$313,000
- **Banker fees** for legacy mortgages (Jan–Apr 2026, e.g. Oakley Crestwood $22,250, Wren Nettleton
  $20,000) are in *4200 Loan origination fees*; they belong in *4100 Mortgage broker fees*. Each
  is followed a week later by that buyer's legacy-mortgage broker payments. 39 rows, $286,362
  (incl. F1).
- **Overdue and early payments on legacy mortgages** use loan-style comments ("Broker percentage
  payment: OVERDUE: …", "Early Finance Repayment Broker Interest") — **a game quirk** — so the
  classifier filed them as loan interest: 16 rows $16,981 (Briar Vance, Noel Ashcombe — their agreement
  IDs match their mortgages) and 28 rows $10,109. They belong in *4110 Mortgage broker interest*.
Profit is unaffected; the split between loans and mortgages, and per-loan/officer figures, are.

### F3 — Refunds for game errors sit away from their loans · presentation · $45,140
Refunds such as "Early Loan Repayment Error" ($12,200, Shea Calloway), "Excessive Payment Amount
Reimbursement" ($2,103, Peyton Holloway), "Failed Payment Refund" ($10,263, Remy Calloway) are in
*5600 Refunds* while the overcharge they reverse sits on the loan as a closing surplus (4310) — e.g.
Shea Calloway's loan shows a $9,498 surplus that was given back. Company profit is right; those
loans' "net to Litt's" is overstated. *Recommend:* link game-error refunds to their loan.

### F4 — Early repayment rescales the remaining instalments (game mechanic, newly confirmed)
After an early repayment the game keeps the **number** of instalments and shrinks each remaining one
— principal and interest alike — by remaining ÷ original principal (Morgan Lockhart: every later
instalment exactly 0.919 of normal after repaying $4,860 early; Onyx Greystone 0.023). 23 loans. The
app's forecast assumes instalments drop off the end instead, so **amounts are right but the timing
is wrong** for these loans. *Recommend:* spread what's owed across the remaining instalments.

### F5 — Overdue penalty: +2% per missed payment, on principal and interest (game mechanic)
On overdue cards both rows rise by 2% for each missed payment (Noel Ashcombe $1,260 → $1,285 → $1,311
→ $1,337 → $1,364). $7,117 collected on 66 rows. The principal part over-credits the receivable,
which then closes as surplus (4310) — total correct, classification approximate.
*Resolved 2026-10-13 (owner): the principal part's penalty is booked as a late fee (4000); see
`docs/qa/QA-2026-10-13.md` #9.*

### F7 — Monthly P&L distortion · presentation
All 2025 manual-loan profit ($176,972, account 4020) is recognised in one entry in June 2026, while
their tax was expensed month by month — so every 2025 month shows a loss and June 2026 is inflated.
*Recommend:* recognise each old loan's result when that loan finished.

### F8 — Tax on investments · cost
Three investments made as "Charge" invoices (Apr 2026: $51,500, $50,000, $77,250) lost $5,206 to
tax. Cash deposits are not taxed. *Recommend:* investors deposit cash.

### F9 — Minor app findings
- The tax report groups by calendar month, so it shows differences of $43–$4,668 that are only
  timing — it should reconcile per State record (see table above).
- The early-overcharge flag isn't rounded to whole dollars ($27,784.23).
- Older levy postings carry cents in individual lines (nets are whole dollars).
- The one-off $11,251 "Vehicle Sale" taxed $11,250 is the DMV transfer (owner).

## What is *not* wrong
No duplicate bank rows, no missing tax, no unpaired mortgage legs, no posting or reversal gaps, no
loan with a stale balance, no levy miscalculation, and no unexplained difference between the books
and the bank.
